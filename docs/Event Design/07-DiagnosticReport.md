This a technical design document for NEMS diagnostic report event. The target audiences are those working on diagnostic report event publisher applications,subscriber applications, and NEMS implementation of diagnostic report events.

## **Background**

The increasing demand for diagnostic services and specialist investigations has placed significant pressure on healthcare systems, resulting in growing waiting lists and delays in patient care. Timely access to diagnostic results is critical for supporting clinical decision-making, prioritising patient pathways, and ensuring appropriate follow-up actions across the continuum of care.

Diagnostic reports are generated by a wide range of clinical services, including pathology laboratories, radiology providers, screening programmes, and specialist assessment services. These reports often contain clinically significant results that must be shared with multiple stakeholders, including the originating service, hospital clinicians, primary care providers, and other health programmes involved in the patient's care.

To support coordinated care delivery, diagnostic report information must be distributed to multiple clinical systems and consumer applications in a timely, secure, and consistent manner. These systems may include regional clinical data repositories, hospital information systems, primary care systems, screening programmes, and national health platforms. The availability of diagnostic results across these systems enables clinicians to make informed decisions, supports continuity of care, and reduces the need for duplicate testing.

The National Event Management Service (NEMS) Diagnostic Report Event has been established as a standard event pattern for the distribution of diagnostic report information across Health New Zealand and partner health organisations. The event enables diagnostic results to be published once and consumed by multiple authorised systems according to their specific business and clinical requirements. This event-driven approach promotes interoperability, reduces point-to-point integrations, and supports near real-time dissemination of diagnostic information.

The initial design and implementation of the Diagnostic Report Event has been informed by priority business use cases, including the FIT for Symptomatic Register (FSR) programme, which requires diagnostic test results to be shared with numerous stakeholders for patient prioritisation and care management. While the first implementation addresses the immediate needs of specific clinical services, the event has been designed as a reusable and extensible national pattern capable of supporting a broad range of future diagnostic reporting scenarios, including pathology, radiology, screening, and other diagnostic domains.

As additional use cases emerge, the Diagnostic Report Event model will continue to evolve to accommodate new clinical workflows, consumer requirements, and interoperability standards, ensuring a scalable and sustainable foundation for diagnostic information exchange across the health sector.

## FSP (FIT for Symptomatic Programme) Eventing Overview
 
![Diagnostic Report Event Process](/docs/Event%20Design/f301325a-c471-44d1-9ca0-4c6ca3b6e05e.png)

The Diagnostic Report Event provides a mechanism for publishing significant lifecycle events associated with the creation and processing of diagnostic reports. Event types correspond to business state changes within a diagnostic workflow and enable authorised consumers to react to those changes in near real time. For example, within the FIT for Symptomatic Programme (FSP), diagnostic reports may generate events such as Ordered, Registered, and Rejected. The Registered event is the most important event type, as it indicates the successful registration of a diagnostic result and typically triggers downstream clinical, operational, and reporting processes.

## **Topic taxonomy**

FThe Diagnostic Report Event topic taxonomy adheres to the standard NEMS topic taxonomy pattern and provides a structured mechanism for event classification, subscription filtering, and message routing.

The generic topic structure is:


`diagnostic/diagnosticreport/[category]/[verb]/v1/[message-type]/[programme]/[LOINC-code]/[placer-facility-code]/[performer-facility-code]`

For FIT for Symptomatic Programme (FSP) diagnostic reports, the topic looks like this:
`diagnostic/diagnosticreport/LAB/registered/v1/HL7/FSP/29771-3/[placer-facility-code]/F36039-G`

## Message payload
The Diagnostic Report Event follows the thick event pattern. 

The payload of the event message for FSR is a full copy of the FSR HL7 message.
